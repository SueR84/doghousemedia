<CaseStep n="01" label="The problem" title="A site that absorbs the routine." surface="secondary">
  Western Rheumatology is four consultant rheumatologists working across two locations in
  Galway. Many of their patients are on long-term medication that has to be monitored, so the
  clinic is asked the same practical things over and over. Where do I go. How do I get an
  appointment. Can I reorder my prescription. What is the drug I have been put on, what dose is
  normal, and what do I watch for.

  The site now takes some of this load, and the practice runs the content themselves. Staff sign in to their
  content management system and publish news posts, articles and alerts without
  waiting for a developer. A new clinic location, a notice about appointment text messages, an
  alert for patients: patients get one place to look for what is current.

<CaseStep n="02" label="Medications" title="Medication knowledge base, and prescriptions in one place." surface="tint">
  The clinic needed one place to keep the detail on each medication, one the practice could edit
  themselves and the website would always match. So the practice maintains a knowledge base of
  more than 50 medications, and the website syncs with it in real time.

  <h3 class="u-h4" style="margin-bottom:var(--pl-s-sm)">Medication Database</h3>

  Fields include: indication, typical dose and range, monitoring and blood tests, side effects
  and infection precautions. Records are tagged by condition, drug class and route of
  administration. The library is searchable, every medication is printable, and each one has a
  unique link, so the clinic can send a patient the one entry they need.

  <h3 class="u-h4" style="margin-bottom:var(--pl-s-sm)">Online Repeat Prescription Ordering</h3>

  Repeat prescriptions work the same way. An existing patient orders through an online form and
  gives their pharmacy at the same time. Requests arrive in one place instead of across phone
  calls and emails, which keeps enquiries to the front desk down and cuts the admin time spent
  chasing them. The conditions the clinic sets around safe prescribing, including up to date
  blood tests, are stated on the same page.

<CaseStep n="03" label="The assistant" title="Bespoke support assistant built around how the clinic works." surface="secondary">
  <div slot="intro">

  We built, trained, tested and maintain a bespoke chatbot solution for the clinic. An assistant
  sits on every page and answers from the Knowledge Base it has been trained on and strict guidelines it has
  been given.

  This is a healthcare website, so accuracy and guardrails were paramount in the
  build and testing phase. The knowledge base is a living document the clinic team can access and
  amend as necessary, and it syncs with the bot on the site. Guardrails and instructions are
  hard-coded in.

  </div>

  There is a feedback loop. Every conversation is logged, and a summary report can be sent to
  the team for any date range. It highlights the conversations where the assistant struggled to
  answer, identifies the gaps in the knowledge base and suggests solutions, so the team can
  decide what to change. The assistant gets better the more it is used, which means fewer calls
  and emails to the admin team. It also takes repeat prescription requests, starting the form
  inside the chat. It is built to be GDPR compliant.

  <h3 class="u-h4" style="margin-bottom:var(--pl-s-sm)">EU AI Act: transparency and oversight built in</h3>

  Under the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), this assistant is an interactive AI system subject to the transparency requirements in Article 50. It is intended to answer general clinic questions and open the repeat-prescription form. It is not intended to diagnose, triage, interpret symptoms or test results, or recommend treatment.

  Patients are clearly told from the start of their first interaction that they are chatting with an AI assistant. The system is operated within a defined administrative scope, using clinic-approved information, fixed safety instructions and human review.

  - **Clear AI disclosure.** Patients are told that they are interacting with AI before beginning a conversation.
  - **Defined purpose and safeguards.** The assistant is instructed not to provide medical advice and uses separate form controls for prescription information.
  - **Grounded in clinic-approved information.** The clinic’s knowledge base and administrative instructions are supplied to the assistant when it answers. It is instructed not to invent an answer when approved information is unavailable.
  - **Data transparency.** Patients are told that chat messages are sent to Mistral AI to generate replies and are directed to the clinic’s privacy information.
  - **Human oversight.** Authorised staff can review logged conversations and identify unanswered questions or inappropriate answers.
  - **Human-led improvement.** Reports help the clinic and Doghouse Media improve the knowledge base, instructions and safeguards.
