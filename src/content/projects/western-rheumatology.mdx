<CaseStep n="01" label="The problem" title="The same questions, arriving one at a time." surface="secondary">
  Western Rheumatology is four consultant rheumatologists working across two locations in
  Galway. Their patients are mostly on long-term medication that has to be monitored, so the
  clinic is asked the same practical things over and over. Where do I go. How do I get an
  appointment. Can I reorder my prescription. What is the drug I have been put on, what dose is
  normal, and what do I watch for.

  Before the site did any of it, every one of those arrived by phone or by email, and each one
  interrupted somebody.
</CaseStep>

<CaseStep n="02" label="What we built" title="One library, and the site keeps up with it.">
  The medication information lives in one place the clinic controls, and the website stays in
  step with it on its own. Nobody maintains a second copy, and no patient reads a version that
  has been superseded.

  On the site it is a library a patient can search. Each entry is tagged by condition, by drug
  class and by how the drug is taken, and opens into what the clinic wants a patient to know:
  what it is for, the usual dose and the range, the blood tests that go with it, and the
  precautions around infection. Every entry prints, and every entry has a link the clinic can
  send to one patient.

<CaseStep n="03" label="The assistant" title="Answers from the clinic's own information." surface="tint">
  An assistant sits on every page and answers from what the clinic has written, not from the
  open internet. Ask it where the clinic is and it comes back with the two locations, Blackrock
  Health Clinic and Bon Secours Hospital, and asks which one you mean.

  It is straight with people about what it is. It says up front that it is an AI assistant, that
  it answers general clinic questions and helps with repeat prescription requests, and that it
  cannot give medical advice. It tells patients that their messages go to Mistral AI to be
  answered, asks them not to type personal medical details into it, and points them at the
  prescription form instead. A clinic cannot afford a chat window that implies more than it is.

<CaseStep n="04" label="Prescriptions" title="Reordering, without ringing." surface="secondary">
  An existing patient orders a repeat prescription from the site and gives their pharmacy at the
  same time, so it goes where it is meant to go. The conditions the clinic sets around safe
  prescribing, including up to date blood tests and the time it takes to prepare one, are stated
  on the same page rather than explained again on the phone.

  Ordering through the assistant itself is the next piece, and the clinic has it marked as
  coming.
</CaseStep>

<CaseStep n="05" label="Where it stands" title="A site that absorbs the routine." surface="tint">
  The practical traffic a specialist clinic generates now has somewhere to go that is open at
  the hour patients think of it. Appointments and prescriptions are requested from the site,
  medication questions are answered in the clinic's own words, and the assistant takes the ones
  that would have been a phone call about a location or an opening time.

  We still look after the site.
</CaseStep>
