<CaseStep n="01" label="The brief" title="Doctors talking to doctors." surface="secondary">
  The idea was simple. Rather than a product film, let the physicians who use the mapping
  software every week explain how they work. Each of the seven runs a training academy at
  their own hospital, in seven different countries, and no two of them do things the same way.

  Two teams at Boston Scientific had a stake in it. Medical education wanted a film per
  centre, so a visiting doctor could see which academy suits them. Marketing had a 2026
  campaign with a new look and needed the series to fit inside it. Two sets of notes on
  every cut.
</CaseStep>

<CaseStep n="02" label="The day" title="Every hour of it used.">
  Getting seven professors from seven hospitals into the same building is the hard bit. Once
  they were, in July 2025, we had them until the end of the day and not a minute longer.
  Interviews ran back to back. The panel was recorded around one table. And Gabor Széplaki,
  our host at the Mater Private, scrubbed in for a live case with a few of the visiting
  professors watching from the side of the lab.

  Six of us on the crew. Lead aprons for the lab. No option of coming back tomorrow.

<CaseStep n="03" label="The edit" title="Seven different films, on purpose." surface="tint">
  Seven long interviews about one piece of software is a trap. Cut them the same way and you
  get the same film seven times over. So before anyone opened a timeline we read every
  transcript and picked a lane for each professor. One on research and how the technology
  got to where it is. One on imaging and anatomy. One on teaching doctors who come from a
  different background. One on safety. And so on.

  Boston Scientific signed off the themes before we cut a frame. That made the reviews
  quick, because every comment was about the story, not what the story should be. The panel
  went out first. The episodes followed one at a time between February and May 2026.

  Each episode carries the name of the professor's own hospital, so watched in order it
  plays like a tour.

<CaseStep n="04" label="Graphics" title="Their brand, their graphics, our call on screen.">
  The brand and the graphic assets for the new campaign came from Boston Scientific. Putting
  them on screen was our job. We animated the title cards and the name straps, kept the
  pacing consistent from film to film, and were fairly ruthless about only using a graphic
  where it helped.

  Boston Scientific's design team have the last word on their look. Ours was making sure
  eight films made over ten months still feel like they were cut in the same week.
