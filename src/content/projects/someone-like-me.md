## The problem

The National Disability Authority runs the Someone Like Me Art Awards every year, inviting primary schools across the country to mark the International Day of Persons with Disabilities on 3 December through art that gets pupils thinking about diversity and disability from an early age. The site's job is straightforward: get schools to sign up and take part, in a format a class teacher can act on quickly. The Authority sets the accessibility standard other Irish organisations are measured against, so its own campaign site had to meet it too, in Irish as well as English, and tested properly rather than assumed compliant.

## What we made

We built the awards site in partnership with Real Nation, who designed and ran the project. The site is in English and Irish, with the Irish text reviewed by a human translator rather than machine-translated and left. Every page went through accessibility testing against WCAG 2.1 AA, and the failures that testing turned up were fixed before launch, not noted and left for a later phase.

## The result

The Someone Like Me site meets the standard for the one organisation in the country whose job is holding everyone else to it. We build to the same discipline for our own site: doghousemedia.ie is tested a level higher again, to WCAG 2.2 AA, because a studio that sells accessibility audits has to pass its own first.
